Commodity windfalls across the fifty states Prototype v0

State Shock Ledger

When world prices for oil, gas, grain or cattle move, income moves between states. This ledger measures each state's gain or loss as a share of its economy, using only public federal data.

How the ledger is built

Each state's windfall is its net position in a commodity multiplied by the change in the world price over the previous twelve months, summed across commodities:

Ws,t = Σc Ns,c × (pc,t − pc,t−12)

For energy, the net position is production minus consumption: crude oil against crude-priced petroleum use (excluding gas liquids, petroleum coke and refinery still gas), and marketed natural gas against total gas use. For agriculture, it is the quantity equivalent of farm cash receipts. Positions are averaged over the three prior years so that the measure moves with prices, following the IMF commodity terms-of-trade method.

  • Figures are gross values at benchmark prices (WTI, Henry Hub, world grain and beef prices). They are an upper bound on income, because part of the windfall goes to owners outside the state and local prices differ from benchmarks.
  • Agriculture covers the producer side only in this prototype. Farm prices pass through to food prices weakly and with delay.
  • Federal offshore production belongs to no state and is reported on its own line.
  • The outlook uses EIA's Short-Term Energy Outlook price path against the average of the last twelve months of prices.
  • Sources: EIA State Energy Data System and daily spot prices; EIA Short-Term Energy Outlook; USDA ERS Farm Income and Wealth Statistics; World Bank commodity prices; BEA state GDP.
