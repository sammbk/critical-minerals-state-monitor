National overview

Each state's exposure to world prices for six critical metals, measured on two sides: as a producer, from the value of its mine output, and as a user, from the metal content of its industries. Release 1.0 covers aluminum, copper, lead, nickel, tin and zinc.

Methods and data

Producer exposure is the value of mine output at the latest 12-month average world price. Copper is summed by state from the leading mines in the USGS Minerals Yearbook 2022 (table 2); zinc is Alaska's output (table 2); nickel is Michigan's Eagle mine, the only U.S. nickel mine (2024). Where USGS withholds a producing state's figure (copper in Montana and Missouri; zinc in Idaho, Missouri, New York and Tennessee; lead in Missouri, Alaska and Idaho) it is excluded, never imputed. Primary aluminum output by state is not compiled in this release; the United States has no tin mine.

User exposure sets each metal's national total at 2024 U.S. apparent consumption (USGS) times the current price, and allocates it across states by each state's real industry output times the 2017 BEA input-output purchases of that metal per dollar of output, excluding the mining, smelting and mill industries themselves. Nickel, lead, zinc and tin share one input-output category and so share one state allocation in this release.

Net effect of a 10% world price rise is one tenth of producer minus user value, as a percent of state GDP. It is a first-round accounting measure, not an estimate of final outcomes.
